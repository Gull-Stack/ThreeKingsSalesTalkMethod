# Email Rules (hard)

These are not style preferences. They are gates. If a draft violates one, rewrite — don't send.

## 1. Never use "I" — in cold and prospect outreach

Sales is about the buyer. "I" relocates the focus to the seller. Rewrite every sentence in "you" form.

- ❌ "I help businesses like yours…"
- ✅ "Your practice is losing patients to…"

**Scope: cold outreach, prospects, and anyone who does not yet know Josh.** It does
NOT apply to a live client, a teammate, or a partner mid-project. There, "I" is the
commitment device — "I'm creating the Google Cloud project today" says a named human
owns it. Scrubbing "I" out of warm client mail reads distant and evasive, which is the
opposite of the goal. (Ruled 2026-08-18 on the Alaina/OnRecord reply.)

## 2. No questions in cold outreach

"Worth a call?" / "Would you like…?" asks permission and surrenders authority. Replace with directive statements.

- ❌ "Would Tuesday at 2 work?"
- ✅ "Tuesday at 2 is open on my side. Let me know if another window is better."

## 3. No assumptions

Do not project values onto the buyer — it reads as presumptuous.

- ❌ "Your work demands excellence…"
- ✅ "The dentists winning in Draper right now all have one thing in common…"

## 4. Short paragraphs

One idea per paragraph. Scannable. White space is a feature.

## 5. Directive close

Tell the reader what happens next. Do not ask permission.

**Scope: cold outreach and prospects.** To a client executive who already said yes to meeting,
give the next step as an *offer with autonomy* ("for your review ahead of time if you want").
See §15 — Josh's own model, which overrides this rule for that audience.

- ❌ "Let me know if you'd like to chat."
- ✅ "Grabbing 15 minutes Thursday to walk you through it."

## 6. Specific > generic

A real 404, a real competitor name, a real number always beats "your site needs work."

## 7. WizGat every claim

For each feature / fact, silently ask "what's so great about that?" until you hit an emotional outcome. Keep the outcome. Cut the feature.

## 8. One CTA

Not two. Not three. One clean next step.

## 9. No emojis to federal or wealth buyers

They read emojis as unserious. Strip them from any federal-retirement or wealth outbound. (Gull-Stack SuperTool outbound can use them sparingly.)

## 10. Signature discipline

Name. Role. Company. Direct contact. Nothing else. No motivational quotes, no legal boilerplate in the visible signature.

## 11. Sound bytes, not sentences

Josh speaks in sound bytes. Short declaratives that land on their own. Write that way.

- ❌ "That's exactly the result we were after — you give him real commitments, he comes back with a real draft."
- ✅ "Exactly the result we were after."

## 12. Say it once

State the thing. Move on. Do not add a follow-up sentence that restates, softens, explains, or re-sells what the previous sentence already said.

- ❌ "Flight Deck is authorized. Consider this the go-ahead — Bones makes the changes. Once it's in, tell him what's off and he'll adjust."
- ✅ "Flight Deck: authorized. Bones makes the access matrix changes and adds Sage."

The reiterating second sentence is the single most common failure. Draft, then delete every sentence whose job is to re-say the one above it.

## 13. Every open action gets a line

Read the inbound and list every action it creates — theirs and yours. None get dropped for elegance. Split them by owner, one line each, verb first. The email is the record of who owes what.

# VOICE — how Josh actually writes

Before drafting anything that goes out over Josh's name, read his real sent mail:
`from:josh@gullstack.com in:sent` — pull two or three to the same audience. The
patterns below are lifted from it. Match them.

**Structure**
- Open with names and an em dash: `Alaina —` / `Spencer, Christian, Alaina —` / `Team,`
- Shouted, colon'd section headers: `THE DECISION:` `ACTION ITEMS` `NEXT SYNC:`
- Action items split by owner, including his own bucket: `Me (GullStack / Bones)`
- Numbered lists under each owner. Verb first.
- Close with one rallying line, then a short sign-off: `Talk Thursday,` / `Josh`

**Register**
- First person, freely. "I'll send the calendar invite." "I had a full audit run."
- Em dashes carry the asides.
- Flat declaratives for the hard parts: "Nothing starts until those two happen."
  "Nothing moves until the answer/process is airtight."
- Bones is **he**, never it.
- Times get proposed with a fallback: "how does Thursday afternoon look — same 3:45
  slot, or earlier if that's better?"
- Warm on the open, never gushing: "Great update." "Great session today."

**Never**
- No villain monologues, no metaphor paragraphs, no story framing. He writes
  operationally and lets the plan do the persuading.
- No self-critique, no hedging — see `feedback_never_self_critique_to_clients`.
- No past-tense intent ("we wanted to") — present tense only.

## 14. A reply is prose, not a document

Shouted headers, bolded labels, and numbered lists are for a recap after a meeting.
On a reply, they read as machine output. Write it as one person talking to another —
short paragraphs, one beat each, actions woven into the sentence that carries them.

- ❌ `**ORDER ENTRY:** the poller comes back up behind that same access change. Order numbering goes first, on a test file.`
- ✅ `Approve it and the poller comes back on too. Order numbering we'll look at together before anything goes near a live file.`

Every action still has to be unmistakable. It just doesn't need a bullet to be one.

**Length test:** a reply to a status email is under 150 words. If it's longer, structure
is doing work that sentences should do.


## 15. Client executive — Josh's model email (David Vitek, 25 Sep 2026)

**Read this before drafting any email to a client's CEO, president or owner.** Josh rewrote my draft
and sent his own. His version is the standard. Thread "Re: Tomorrow's Meeting", Candlelight Homes.

**What Josh sent (verbatim):**

> David,
>
> Thank you for your time and energy.
>
> Knowing our time will fly by, It seems it might be helpful to share the marketing presentation
> from yesterday - (Here is the presentation) for your review ahead of time if you want.
>
> The focus is to help solve these challenges fast:
>
> **1. The data is disparate**
> - **Eight systems, seven answers** for January 2026 leads. Not connected/communicating.
> - **Most leads are typed in by hand.** 1,695 of 4,901 were recorded automatically.
> - **Meta leads are retyped into Lasso.** The automatic connection exists but is not set up.
> - **Google Ads shows 0 conversions.** Tracking is down.
>
> **2. The buyer journey has some gaps**
> - **Meta leads skip the website.** Since May, buyers never see the homes or prices.
> - **Three website forms stopped working** while ads kept sending people to them.
> - **No live calendar, no monthly payment.** Every path ends in a form and a wait.
> - **Three of six nearby builders** offer three of the four things buyers look for. Candlelight offers one.
>
> ***Building the one single source of truth will be so fun and helpful for your success and performance.***
>
> [signature block only]

**What my draft did wrong, line by line:**

| My draft | Josh | The move |
|---|---|---|
| "Thank you for your time yesterday." | "Thank you for your time and energy." | Thank the **person**, not the calendar slot. |
| (nothing) | "Knowing our time will fly by, it seems…" | **Voss label of THEIR constraint** before any ask. "It seems" labels; it does not claim. |
| "This link opens the presentation directly, no passcode, and is yours alone:" | "…might be helpful… for your review ahead of time if you want." | **Offer with autonomy.** Our plumbing (passcodes, access) is not his concern; the link sits in a parenthetical. |
| "The facts are below, ahead of 1:00." | "The focus is to help solve these challenges fast:" | **Guide states the plan as the hero's win** (Miller). |
| "The buyer journey is suffering" | "The buyer journey has some gaps" | **Neutral words, bold facts.** Blame the system, never a person — someone named in the facts may be in the room. |
| "Tracking is not set up." | "Tracking is down." | Same: describe the state, not who failed to act. |
| "The fix: one single source of truth. Every source in one record…" | "**Building the one single source of truth will be so fun and helpful for your success and performance.**" | **Close on HIS success, warmly (WizGat).** Enthusiasm is FOR him, not about us. Bold it. |
| "Josh" + typed signature | signature block only | Name once. |

**The checklist — every client-executive email:**
1. First line thanks the person ("your time and energy", "your trust", "your candour").
2. Second beat labels their constraint or feeling ("Knowing…", "It seems…") before any ask.
3. Every ask is an offer: "might be helpful", "if you want", "ahead of time". No commands, no mechanics.
4. One line of purpose in their terms: "The focus is to help solve these challenges fast."
5. Facts bold and specific; adjectives neutral ("has some gaps", "is down", "not connected"). No person blamed.
6. Last line is their win, warm and bold. No "let us know", no recap.
7. No name above the signature.
8. **Links**: never end a sentence with a bare URL — the mail client folds the full stop into it. Put the
   link in an anchor or on its own line, and **open the link exactly as sent** before calling it done
   (David's sign-in link broke this way; fixed server-side, PR #85, candlelight-homes).
9. **Links are the bare, real URL as plain text** (Josh, 29 Sep 2026: "never link to google link - just link in gmail to
   the actual link"). No `<a href>` anchors: Gmail rewrites them through a google.com/url redirect. Gmail
   auto-links a plain `https://…` on its own. After saving the draft, assert 0 `<a ` and 0 `google.com/url` in the raw.
