# Email Rules (hard)

These are not style preferences. They are gates. If a draft violates one, rewrite — don't send.

## 1. Never use "I"

Sales is about the buyer. "I" relocates the focus to the seller. Rewrite every sentence in "you" form.

- ❌ "I help businesses like yours…"
- ✅ "Your practice is losing patients to…"

## 2. No questions in cold outreach

"Worth a call?" / "Would you like…?" asks permission and surrenders authority. Replace with directive statements.

- ❌ "Would Tuesday at 2 work?"
- ✅ "Tuesday at 2 is open on my side. Let me know if another window is better."

## 3. No assumptions

Do not project values onto the buyer — it reads as presumptuous.

- ❌ "Your work demands excellence…"
- ✅ "The dentists winning in Draper right now all have one thing in common…"

## 4. Short paragraphs

One idea per paragraph. Scannable. White space is a feature.

## 5. Directive close

Tell the reader what happens next. Do not ask permission.

- ❌ "Let me know if you'd like to chat."
- ✅ "Grabbing 15 minutes Thursday to walk you through it."

## 6. Specific > generic

A real 404, a real competitor name, a real number always beats "your site needs work."

## 7. WizGat every claim

For each feature / fact, silently ask "what's so great about that?" until you hit an emotional outcome. Keep the outcome. Cut the feature.

## 8. One CTA

Not two. Not three. One clean next step.

## 9. No emojis to federal or wealth buyers

They read emojis as unserious. Strip them from any federal-retirement or wealth outbound. (Gull-Stack SuperTool outbound can use them sparingly.)

## 10. Signature discipline

Name. Role. Company. Direct contact. Nothing else. No motivational quotes, no legal boilerplate in the visible signature.
