# Application: Capital Wealth → Federal Employees

Context from the Ann meeting, 2026-04-08. Ann is the external federal-benefits expert advising CW.

## The villain (Miller)

Not "retirement planning is hard." Specific, hateable, root-cause:

- **2011 Sequestration** — Congress cut 10% across all agencies. HR was gutted, centralized, relocated out of the agencies.
- Federal employees **literally cannot talk to HR**. They get: an email address → ticket number → generic response 4-5 weeks later.
- The retirement "counselor" sends a link to an online application. No counseling.
- **YOYO — You're On Your Own.**
- COVID compounded it: no more "Joe in the break room" who knew stuff.
- 2026 DOGE chaos made it worse.
- **Moral layer:** employees feel they *should* know their benefits, don't, and feel dumb asking.

That is the villain. Name it. Externalize the fight.

## The hero and their wound

The federal employee, mid-to-late career, with a spouse often in the private sector. The wound is not ignorance — it's the isolation of being expected to navigate FERS / CSRS / TSP / FEHB / FEGLI alone, with no one to ask.

## Accusation audit openers (Voss)

- "You've probably been sent to three people who all told you to check OPM's website."
- "You're probably skeptical of advisors who claim to know federal benefits — most don't."
- "You probably feel like you should already know this stuff, and it's uncomfortable to admit you don't."

## The insight that crosses the threshold (Belfort)

Speak something they *couldn't have known, or felt but never said aloud:*

- "Your GRB pension estimate can be off by hundreds of dollars a month — you just want verifiable numbers."
- "Less than 5% of feds take the $162.50/month HSA government contribution. The high-deductible plan is only $3,000 out of pocket — not the $7,500 you've been picturing."
- "Most feds are on the most expensive FEHB plan and could save hundreds a month without losing coverage."
- "Most feds have no idea when they *can* retire. Not when they want to — when the math actually lets them."

Any one of these, delivered calmly, crosses the threshold of action.

## Buyer psychology (from Ann)

- **15 touchpoints of trust** before they let CW manage their money. Patience is the strategy.
- **No self-serve tools.** Do not give them a calculator. Take them *just* far enough that they realize they can't do this alone — then the scheduling CTA.
- **"Fish chasing the boat."** Make CW so attractive they come to you.
- **Nordstrom, not Dillard's.** Same product, delivered better.
- **Spouse in the meeting.** The fed takes time off easily. The private-sector spouse may not. Use: "Would it be important for your spouse to understand this too?"
- **Key objection: "I've done my own research, I think I'm good."** → Respond with a specific insight proving they missed something.
