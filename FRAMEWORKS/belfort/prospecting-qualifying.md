# Prospecting & Qualifying — who's worth the line

Closing power is wasted on the wrong prospect. Before you invest the full Straight Line, qualify. Belfort's frame: sort prospects into **buyers, lookie-loos (the curious/never-buyers), and people in pain** — and spend your energy on real buyers and the in-pain.

## The qualifying pass (do this early, in intelligence-gathering)

Establish, through a logical sequence of questions, that the prospect has:

1. **Need / pain** — a real problem your product solves. No pain, no urgency, no sale. Surface it and confirm it.
2. **Belief layer** — what they already think about products like yours, and about buying. Tells you which certainties start low.
3. **Standards / past experience** — what they've used before, what worked, what burned them. Reveals objections before they're raised.
4. **Financial ability** — can they actually afford it. Don't presume; qualify it respectfully so you don't close someone who can't fund it (and don't disqualify someone who can).
5. **Pain + decision authority + timeline** — how much it hurts, whether *this* person decides, and when they'd move.

## The point of qualifying

- **Tailoring.** Every answer feeds the presentation. You present *only* what raises *this* buyer's low certainties.
- **Threshold read.** Qualifying reveals whether they're a low- or high-action-threshold buyer, so you know how much certainty you must build.
- **Time discipline.** Politely de-prioritize lookie-loos. The fortune is in spending full intensity on qualified prospects, not in closing everyone.

## Don't over-qualify into rapport loss

Qualifying questions still ride the "I care / I want to know" tone. It's a conversation that *feels* like genuine interest, not an interrogation. The prospect should feel understood, not screened. Rapport and qualifying happen in the same breath.

## The cardinal rule

**Never prejudge a prospect's ability to buy from appearances** — but **do** qualify it through questions. Assumptions lose deals at both ends: you'll under-sell someone who could buy, and over-invest in someone who can't.
