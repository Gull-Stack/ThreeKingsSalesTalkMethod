# Looping — turning objections into closes

**An objection is almost never a real "no." It is a request for more certainty on one of the Three Tens.** "I need to think about it" doesn't mean they need to think — it means a certainty is below a 10 and they haven't told you which. Looping is the technique for converting that hesitation into a higher certainty and then re-closing.

**Never answer an objection literally.** Acknowledge it, deflect into raising the weakest certainty, then close again — each pass with slightly more tonal certainty and slightly less perceived risk.

## The loop, step by step

1. **Hear it out + acknowledge** (sincerity / "I care" tone). "I hear you, and that's a fair point." Never argue, never get defensive. (This is where Voss's labeling slots in cleanly.)
2. **Deflect into a certainty-raise.** Don't address the literal words — pivot to the underlying certainty. "Let me ask you this —" then re-build product / you / company.
3. **Take the certainty higher than before.** Each loop should leave them *more* certain than the last, not just back to baseline. Add a new proof, a sharper benefit, a vision of the outcome.
4. **Re-close.** Ask for the order again, assumptively. "So with that handled, let's go ahead and get you started."
5. **Repeat.** Expect 2–5 loops. Most sales are made after the 3rd–5th attempt. Stay warm, stay certain, keep lowering risk.

## The two-part structure of every loop

- **Raise certainty** (logical proof + emotional vision on the weak ten), then
- **Lower the threshold of action** (reduce risk: guarantee, small first step, "you can't lose here"). Certainty up + risk down = they cross.

## Worked examples

**"I need to think about it."**
→ "I totally understand — you want to be sure this is right, and that's smart. Let me ask you: on the [product] itself, does it make sense that this solves [their stated pain]? … Good. So really the only question is whether *now* is the time — and here's why it is …" (raise product + add urgency, re-close).

**"It's too expensive."**
→ "I get it — and price matters. Let me ask you though: set the price aside for one second — if it were free, is this the right fit for what you're trying to do? … It is. Then what we're really talking about is whether the return justifies it, and here's the math …" (separates fit from price, raises value certainty, re-close).

**"I want to talk to my spouse / partner."**
→ Acknowledge it's the right instinct, then surface the hidden number: "Makes sense — and when you walk them through it, what's the *one* thing they'll want to be sure of?" Whatever they name *is* the low certainty. Raise it now so they can sell it at home.

**"Let me do some research / compare options."**
→ Reasonable-man tone: "Smart — you should. Here's exactly what to look for so you're comparing the right things …" then frame the comparison so your solution wins, raise company certainty, re-close.

## The hard rules of looping

- Never answer the literal objection; answer the certainty behind it.
- Never let frustration leak into your tonality — that signals weakness and confirms their doubt.
- Never give up after one "no." The first "no" is the start of the sale.
- Every loop must *advance* — more certainty, less risk — never just repeat.
- Know when it's a true no: if certainty is genuinely high on all three and they still won't move, respect it, leave the door open, don't grind a real "no" into resentment.
