# Tonality — the ~90%

Belfort's claim: the **words are ~10%** of your impact. **Tonality (~33%) and body language (~57%)** carry the rest in person; on the phone tonality carries almost everything. The same sentence, said in different tones, lands as confidence, weakness, or threat. Master the tones and you control the emotional state of the room.

In writing (email, copy, scripts), tonality is encoded through **rhythm, sentence length, punctuation, and word choice** — short declaratives read as certainty; trailing phrases read as warmth or scarcity. Write the tone in.

## The core influencing tones

Belfort teaches ~29 tonal patterns; these are the high-leverage ones to wield deliberately:

- **"I care / I want to know"** — soft, curious, a slight upward lilt. The rapport tone. Signals genuine interest. Open with it; return to it whenever you ask a question.
- **Absolute certainty** — flat, declarative, no lilt, slows slightly at the key word. "This *will* work." Certainty is contagious; this is the spine of the whole system.
- **Scarcity / "this is hot"** — lowered volume, leaning-in urgency, implication of limited time or limited spots. Makes the offer feel alive and perishable.
- **Reasonable man** — matter-of-fact, friend-to-friend, "look, between us…" Disarms; reframes you as an ally giving straight advice, not a salesperson pushing.
- **Mystery / intrigue** — hushed, pulled-in, "lean closer." Spikes curiosity and attention before a key reveal.
- **Implied obviousness ("of course")** — said as if the conclusion is self-evident and any reasonable person already agrees. Lowers resistance to the next ask.
- **Utter sincerity / "I feel your pain"** — slows down, drops in pitch, genuine. Used when acknowledging an objection before looping. Bridges to Voss's labeling.
- **The "money" tone (deliberate, weighted)** — slow, deliberate cadence on price/commitment so it lands as worth-it, not as a flinch.
- **The "money on the table" / scarcity-of-you** — your time and attention are valuable; you're choosing to spend them on this prospect.

## Patterns, not monotone

- **Inflection** — end key statements *downward* (certainty) and questions with a soft *upward* lilt (care). Avoid the up-talk that makes statements sound like questions; it kills authority.
- **Pace + pause** — slow down and pause *before* the most important word. The pause creates anticipation and weight. Rushing reads as nervous; the right pause reads as control.
- **Micro-agreement tones** — soft "mm-hm," "right," "exactly" to keep the prospect feeling heard and the line moving.

## In writing — encode tone like this

- Certainty → short sentences. Periods, not maybes. "This works. Here's why."
- Scarcity → time/quantity language + tight rhythm. "Three spots. They close Friday."
- Care → a calibrated question or a labeled feeling near the open.
- Reasonable man → "Look —", "Here's the straight version —".
- Never let written tone wander into hype. Certainty ≠ exclamation points. It's the absence of hedging.
