# The Syntax of a Sale — the Straight Line itself

A sale runs on a fixed sequence. Skip a step and the close collapses. The line goes:
**Open → Build rapport / gather intelligence → Transition to the body → Present → Loop (handle objections by raising thresholds) → Close.**

Stay on the line. The prospect will try to wander (tangents, premature price questions, "let me think"). Acknowledge briefly, then steer back to the line. Friendly, firm, always advancing.

## 1. The open (first 4 seconds)

Establish, instantly and through tonality, that you are: **sharp as a tack, enthusiastic as hell, an expert in your field.** (See `tonality.md`.) Be brief. State who you are and the reason for the contact with energy and absolute certainty. Do not over-explain. Earn the right to ask questions.

## 2. Build rapport + gather intelligence

Two jobs at once:
- **Rapport** — get them to like and trust you, fast, using the "I care / I want to know" tone and active listening. Rapport is not chit-chat; it's the prospect feeling *understood*.
- **Intelligence** — ask a logical, escalating sequence of questions to learn their needs, pains, values, prior experiences, decision criteria, and threshold. You cannot tailor a presentation you didn't gather intel for. The best closers ask, then *shut up and listen*.

Use both **logic and emotion** questions. Logic surfaces the criteria; emotion surfaces the real driver (the pain behind the pain). You are mapping where their three certainties currently sit.

## 3. Transition

A clean bridge from intelligence-gathering into the presentation: "Based on everything you just told me, this is exactly what I'd recommend, and here's why…" Signals you listened and that what follows is tailored, not canned.

## 4. Present

Lay out the solution as the inevitable answer to *their* stated pain. Keep it tight and benefit-led — every feature followed by its emotional outcome (WizGat: "which means…"). Build all three certainties as you go (product first, then you, then company). Do **not** info-dump; present only what raises the certainties that matter to *this* buyer. End the presentation by asking for the order — directly.

## 5. Loop

They will not say yes the first time. Expect 2–5 objections. Each is a request for more certainty, not a "no." Loop it (see `looping.md`): acknowledge, raise the relevant certainty, close again, slightly increasing tonality and lowering the perceived risk each pass.

## 6. Close

Direct, assumptive, certain. Tell them the next step as a statement, not a question. "Here's what we'll do…" The close is not a separate event you build up to — you've been closing the whole way. The final ask is just the natural last inch of the line.

## The rule that governs all six steps

**Stay on the line.** Every sentence either raises a certainty or moves toward the close. If a sentence does neither, cut it. When the prospect drifts off-line, you don't follow them — you acknowledge in one beat and pull the conversation back.
