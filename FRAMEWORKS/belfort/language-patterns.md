# Language Patterns — the words that move

Words are only ~10%, but the *right* words paired with the right tonality compound the effect. These are reusable patterns from the Straight Line — for scripts, calls, email, and copy.

## Power transitions (keep the line straight)

- "Let me ask you this —" (pivot from objection into a certainty-raise)
- "Based on everything you just told me…" (intelligence → presentation transition)
- "Here's the bottom line…" / "Here's the straight version…" (reasonable-man reset)
- "Does that make sense?" → soft confirmation that advances; collect micro-yeses.
- "So with that handled, here's what we'll do…" (re-close after a loop)

## Certainty language

- State outcomes as facts, not hopes: "This *will*…" not "This *should* probably…"
- Strip hedges: cut "I think," "kind of," "maybe," "just," "hopefully." Each one drops your certainty tone a notch.
- "The only real question is whether…" (narrows the decision to one resolvable point you then resolve).

## Pain + future-pacing

- "Where does that leave you in [timeframe] if nothing changes?" (amplify cost of inaction)
- "Picture this a year from now, once [outcome] is handled…" (future-pace the pleasure)
- "You felt that, right?" (confirm the emotional hit landed)

## Insight openers (cross the action threshold)

- "Here's what most people in your position never realize…"
- "What you're describing isn't actually a [X] problem — it's a [deeper Y] problem." (the reframe insight)
- Earn the threshold-crossing by saying the thing they felt but never put into words.

## Micro-yes ladder

Stack small agreements before the close — each "yes" makes the next easier:
- "Makes sense so far?" → "You'd agree that's the real issue?" → "And solving it is worth doing right?" → close.

## The directive close (never ask permission)

- "Here's what we'll do —" (statement, not a question)
- "I'll get you set up; the first step is…" (assumptive)
- Avoid permission-seeking closes ("Would you maybe want to possibly…") — they collapse the certainty you just built.

## Hard rules (these echo the skill's EMAIL-RULES)

- **"You," not "I."** Keep the buyer the subject.
- **No hedges, no fillers** — they read as low certainty.
- **One idea per sentence/paragraph** — rhythm carries tonality in writing.
- **Every feature gets a "which means…"** (WizGat) — no naked facts.
- **End on a directive,** never a request for permission.
