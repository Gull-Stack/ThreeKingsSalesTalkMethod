Every buyer experiences purchase as a story. If you cast your company as the hero, you lose. The customer is the hero. You are the guide.

## The 7-part framework (SB7)

1. **A character** — the customer. They want something.
2. **Has a problem** — external (the surface issue), internal (how it makes them feel), philosophical (why it's wrong that this problem exists).
3. **Meets a guide** — you. The guide shows two things: *empathy* ("I understand") and *authority* ("I've solved this for others").
4. **Who gives them a plan** — a clear, 3–4 step path forward.
5. **And calls them to action** — direct CTA (book the call) + transitional CTA (download the guide, take the quiz).
6. **That helps them avoid failure** — name the stakes of doing nothing.
7. **And ends in success** — paint the specific aspirational identity.

## Name the villain

Heroes need villains. Villains must be:
- **Singular** — one enemy, not a list.
- **Relatable** — the buyer has met it.
- **Root cause** — not a symptom.
- **Hateable** — the buyer already dislikes it.

Naming the villain externalizes the fight. The buyer is no longer fighting their own inadequacy — they're fighting a real antagonist, and you're the one with the weapon.

**Capital Wealth federal villain (canonical):** complexity + being left on your own + feeling dumb for not knowing. Named origin: the 2011 Sequestration that gutted agency HR.

## Grunt test

Three questions a stranger should answer in 5 seconds on your landing page:
1. What do you offer?
2. How will it make my life better?
3. What do I need to do to buy it?

If any of the three is unclear, the page fails.

## The guide posture

- Never self-promote. Promote the hero's future.
- "People don't care how good Capital Wealth is. They care: can you solve MY problem?"
- Authority is shown through specifics (results, testimonials, numbers) — not adjectives.

## When Miller wins alone

At the top of the funnel — websites, ads, lead magnets, first-touch emails. Before the buyer knows you. Miller wins the click; Voss wins the call; Belfort wins the close.
