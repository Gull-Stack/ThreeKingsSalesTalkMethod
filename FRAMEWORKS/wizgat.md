# WizGat — "What's So Great About That"

Origin: QVC / direct response. If you can't answer "what's so great about that?" after a feature, the feature does not belong in the pitch.

## The drill

After every factual claim, silently ask: *what's so great about that?* Keep asking until you hit an emotional outcome. That outcome is what goes in the copy — not the feature.

## Examples

| Feature | What's so great about that? | What actually goes in copy |
|---|---|---|
| "We use FERS-specific optimization software" | → You won't miss a benefit | → You won't miss a benefit |
| "Your pension estimate is off by $400/month" | → You'd retire later than you had to | → "You could retire sooner than you thought" |
| "HSA with $162.50/month government contribution" | → Free money most feds leave on the table | → "$1,950 a year most of your coworkers never touch" |
| "We host on Vercel" | → (cut it — the buyer doesn't care) | *(cut)* |

## The rule

If WizGat lands at a technical benefit, keep going. You're not done. The chain must end in one of: fear relieved, pride earned, time returned, status gained, or control regained. If it doesn't, the fact is ego-copy.

## Why this ties the three kings together

- **Voss labels the emotional outcome.** WizGat tells you which emotion.
- **Belfort raises thresholds with certainty.** WizGat makes the certainty matter.
- **Miller paints the aspirational identity.** WizGat is the atomic unit that identity is built from.

Every sentence in your copy that doesn't survive WizGat is weight your buyer has to carry to the close. Cut it.
